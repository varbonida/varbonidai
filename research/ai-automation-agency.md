# AI Automation Agency Niches (10 Projects)

Source: `10 automation projects.txt` — Taglish script pitching 10 AI automation project ideas to freelancers/agencies. Kept here as build requirements/reference, not just summary.

## The core pattern

All 10 projects sell **revenue protection**, not new revenue: missed calls, dead leads, unpaid invoices, churning customers, missed RFP deadlines, undetected contract risk, unreviewed sales calls, undocumented knowledge. The repeatable architecture is: pull from an existing data source the client already has → AI drafts/scores/classifies/converses → escalate high-stakes cases to a human → deliver through a channel the client already uses (SMS, email, dashboard, CRM, doc).

## Stated selection criteria (from the creator)

- Must save the client measurable time or money — no numbers to show, no offer.
- Buildable in 4-8 weeks using Zapier/Make/n8n/any CRM, or agentic tools (Claude Code/Codex-style), with clear scope. Faster is fine as long as quality holds.
- Low competition — problems clients know they have, but nobody's really offering a fix.

## 1. Dead Lead Reactivation / Database Reactivation

**Problem:** Almost every established business has forgotten about its old leads list — hundreds or thousands of people who inquired months or years ago, never closed, and were dropped.

**Workflow:**
- System goes through the client's old lead database.
- Segments the leads.
- Starts a personalized conversation per lead via SMS or email — explicitly **not** a blast; must be a real two-way conversation.
- AI handles replies, answers questions.
- AI books qualified/interested leads directly onto the sales team's calendar.

**Why clients buy it:** Zero acquisition cost — the leads are already paid for/owned. Any deal that comes out of the campaign is pure recovered revenue.

**Monetization:** Can be sold on partial performance pricing — a setup fee plus a percentage of revived/closed deals. Stated as something clients rarely say no to.

## 2. AI Voice Receptionist for Missed Calls

**Problem:** Most AI chatbot builders focus on text, but nobody's solving missed phone calls. For many local businesses the phone *is* the business (dentists, clinics, law firms, auto shops, etc.). Studies show most small-business calls go unanswered, and most of those callers came from Google or another online source — i.e. paid-for or hard-won attention that's being wasted.

**Workflow:**
- Voice AI agent picks up when the actual staff doesn't answer.
- Agent answers caller questions, gathers caller details.
- Books appointments directly to the calendar.
- Sends a summary via SMS to the business owner.
- Also covers calls that come in after business hours — no missed call or inquiry goes unhandled.

**Pitch line (verbatim from script):** "How many calls did you miss last week? How much was that potential sale worth? That's what they're losing every month."

**Selling point:** Voice AI quality has gotten very strong "since last year," and most small businesses haven't adopted it yet.

## 3. RFP and Tender Response Automation

**Problem:** Companies whose customers are government agencies, hospitals, universities, or large corporations must respond to RFPs (requests for proposal). These documents are brutal — huge numbers of questions, strict mandated formatting, tight deadlines. Some teams spend 40-80 hours producing a single response, and much of what they write repeats across different RFPs. Teams have to manually dig through old documents to reuse answers.

**Workflow:**
- Build a system holding a library of the company's past RFP responses, certifications, case studies, and policies.
- When a new RFP comes in, AI extracts the questions.
- AI drafts answers pulled from the library, in the format the new RFP requires.
- Anything that genuinely needs human judgment gets flagged/routed to a person instead of auto-answered.

**Value:** Cuts response time roughly in half (e.g. 80 hours → 40 hours), which lets the client bid on more RFPs, not just finish existing ones faster.

**Note from creator:** Called out as one of the **highest-ticket** automations on the whole list, and one of the least-offered.

## 4. Accounts Receivable and Payment Chasing

**Problem:** The "awkward truth" for small businesses — they're rarely short on sales, they're short on collected cash. Invoices go unpaid for 30/60/90 days. Chasing payments is uncomfortable and nobody on staff wants to do it.

**Workflow:**
- Automation watches the client's accounting software (Xero, QuickBooks, or whatever they use).
- When an invoice goes overdue, an escalating sequence starts automatically:
  1. Friendly reminder.
  2. A firmer reminder.
  3. A payment plan offer.
- All messages are AI-written to match the specific client relationship and the invoice size — tone must stay gentle/polite even as it escalates.
- Payments get matched/reconciled automatically as they come in.
- Invoices that are severely overdue get escalated to a human with full context, rather than kept in the automated sequence indefinitely.

**Pitch line (verbatim):** "How much money is sitting in your unpaid invoices right now?" — framed as likely a 6-figure answer, after which the client stops questioning your rate.

**Positioning note (explicit):** You are not selling automation here — you're selling peace of mind and better sleep, because cash flow is what actually stresses business owners out.

## 5. Sales Call Quality Analysis (+ Support QA extension)

**Problem:** Companies that record sales calls mostly never review them — sales managers don't have time to review 50+ calls a week. Coaching ends up based on "vibes." Nobody actually knows why top reps close deals and others don't.

**Workflow:**
- Every recorded call gets transcribed.
- AI evaluates whether the rep followed the script, handled the pricing objection properly, asked about/offered discounts appropriately, etc.
- Each call gets scored against the company's own sales playbook.
- Each sales rep gets a weekly coaching summary.
- The sales manager gets a dashboard showing why deals are being lost.

**Extension (explicit):** Same pipeline can be applied to customer support calls for quality assurance and compliance purposes.

**Target:** Any company with 5+ people using phones for sales or support. Framed as high demand, low competition.

## 6. Customer Feedback Mining / Voice of Customer Analysis

**Problem:** Businesses get overwhelmed by feedback scattered across reviews, survey responses, support tickets, chat logs, cancellation reasons, etc. — unstructured, high-volume, and mostly never actually read.

**Workflow:**
- Build a pipeline that pulls feedback from every one of those sources into one place.
- AI tags each comment by topic and sentiment.
- Generates a monthly report showing what's trending, what customers keep requesting, and the actual reasons behind cancellations.

**Target:** Especially useful for product companies, e-commerce brands, SaaS — helps leadership see what to fix and where to focus to grow sales.

**Monetization (explicit):** Pitched as a **retainer**, not a one-time project — feedback keeps coming every month, so this is recurring revenue with compounding insight value for the client.

## 7. Contract Review Automation / Red-Flag Detection

**Problem:** SMBs constantly receive contracts — vendor agreements, client contracts, leases, NDAs — but usually don't actually read them closely. Sending every contract to a lawyer is expensive, so most get skipped/signed unreviewed.

**Workflow:**
- When any contract comes in, AI reviews it against a checklist the company's own lawyer helped create.
- Checks payment terms, liability caps, auto-renewal traps, termination clauses, and more.
- Produces a plain-English summary with red flags ranked by severity.
- Client knows exactly what to ask about before signing.

**Positioning note (explicit, important):** This does **not** replace the client's lawyer — it's a first-level filter. The lawyer still gets called in when actually needed. Framed as something that could make both the client and their lawyer happy.

## 8. E-commerce Product Listing Factory

**Problem:** Online sellers with large catalogs hit a scaling wall — every product needs a title, description, SEO keywords, bullet points, image alt text, formatted differently per marketplace, sometimes in multiple languages, across large numbers of SKUs that never quite get finished.

**Workflow:**
- Client provides raw product data: supplier specs, a few photos, basic details.
- Workflow generates complete listings matched to the brand's tone, SEO-optimized, correctly formatted per platform (website, Amazon, wherever they sell).
- If multiple languages are needed, that's built into the same pipeline.
- When price, stock levels, or specs change in the client's master sheet, listings across all platforms update automatically.

**Sales tactic (explicit):** Demo it — take at least 10 of the client's real products, generate listings, compare against what they currently have. Once they see it done in minutes vs. their team's full day of work, it's described as effectively a closed deal.

## 9. SOP and Training Doc Generator

**Problem:** Business owners know their processes need documenting (SOPs, training materials, onboarding guides) but writing it all manually is tedious and unpopular, so it doesn't get done. Knowledge stays only in people's heads — and disappears when they resign or leave. Undocumented processes fall apart without warning.

**Workflow:**
- Instead of asking someone to write everything out, capture the knowledge the easy way: record the employee's screen while they do the actual task, with narration.
- Alternative: employee answers interview questions over a call instead.
- AI generates a step-by-step SOP from that recording/interview — with sections, screenshots, and a checklist.
- Output gets saved into the company's library — SharePoint, Google Docs, Google Drive, etc.
- After a few weeks of this, a complete operations manual emerges.

**Target buyers (explicit):** Any business that's scaling, hiring, franchising, or selling something — properly documented processes improve day-to-day operational efficiency and save time/money.

## 10. Churn Prediction and Win-Back System

**Problem:** Every recurring-revenue business (SaaS, gyms, agencies, insurance, etc.) loses customers to sudden cancellations that are hard to catch in time. But churn is rarely truly sudden — customers show warning signs first: usage drops, stop logging in, or start filing nothing but complaint support tickets.

**Workflow:**
- Build a system that checks the client's own data for those churn signals.
- AI scores each customer for churn risk and takes action before they actually cancel.
- High-risk customers get an automated, personalized check-in, potentially with a win-back offer.
- Alternative: customer gets flagged to an account manager, with full context on what changed, for a human-led reach-out.
- Also covers failed-payment sequences (dunning) so customers don't silently drop off from a failed card rather than an active cancel.

**Positioning note (explicit):** Retaining an existing customer is easier than acquiring a new one, and reduced churn compounds into real money at scale. You are not selling a workflow here — you are selling **revenue protection**. Price accordingly.

## Productised alternative: sell the system, not the service

A separate 2026-05-11 thread explored the same skill sold a different way:
automation systems packaged as digital products rather than delivered as
client engagements. Tooling named was GoHighLevel, Zapier, Make, Notion,
Airtable, and ChatGPT. The idea taken furthest was an Agency White-Label
Suite, with a build plan, pricing strategy, and a 30-day launch plan.

Worth holding next to the ten patterns above, because the economics are
opposite. Agency work is high-touch, priced per client, and stops earning when
you stop working. A productised system is built once and sold repeatedly, but
carries support, updates, and marketing that agency work does not, and it only
works if the buyer can install it without you.

Nothing was built. Recorded so the option is not rediscovered from scratch.

## Caveats — treat as sales narrative, not researched fact

- "Clients will pay 6 digits" — currency never specified; given the Filipino audience it's likely PHP, not USD, which changes the deal size by an order of magnitude.
- No case studies or metrics tied to these specific 10 projects (the creator's "100+ workflows built" claim is about broader past work, not these ideas).
- No mention anywhere of data privacy/compliance handling — a real gap for the RFP (government/hospital/university client data) and contract review (legal liability) projects specifically.
- Pricing model only explicitly specified for 2 of 10 (performance-based for lead reactivation, retainer for feedback mining); the rest are unstated in the script.
- "MIG" in the transcript is almost certainly a mis-transcription of Make.com; "cloud code, codecs" is almost certainly Claude Code / Codex — auto-caption artifacts, not real distinct tools.

## Open questions before building any of these

- Real currency/value behind the "6 digits" claim.
- Which single niche matches an actual client or channel I already have access to (script explicitly says not to build all 10).
- Compliance/liability requirements for the higher-risk projects (RFP, contract review, AR/payment data, churn/usage data).
- Actual per-project technical complexity — voice AI, RAG-style document retrieval, and classification pipelines are not equally "easy," despite the script treating them as interchangeable 4-8 week builds.
